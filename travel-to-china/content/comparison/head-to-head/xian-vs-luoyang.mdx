# Xi'an vs Luoyang – Which Ancient Capital Wins?
 
 Xi'an and Luoyang are China's two greatest historical heavyweights. Between them, they served as the imperial capital for over 2,000 years — Xi'an for 1,100+ years (13 dynasties), Luoyang for 1,500+ years (13 dynasties). They're connected by 2.5-hour high-speed rail, making them natural partners in any history-focused China itinerary.
 
 But if you can only visit one, this guide helps you decide.

 ## Detailed Comparison
 
 ### The Big Attractions
 
 **Xi'an's Terracotta Warriors** is the single most famous archaeological site in China — 8,000 life-sized clay soldiers buried with the First Emperor in 210 BC. It's a genuine Wonder of the World. The sheer scale, the individual facial features of every warrior, the bronze chariots — it stops you in your tracks.
 
 **Luoyang's Longmen Grottoes** is one of humanity's greatest artistic achievements — 100,000 Buddhist statues carved into a kilometre-long cliff face over 400 years. The 17-metre Vairocana Buddha, completed under Empress Wu Zetian in 676 AD, is a masterpiece of spiritual sculpture. It's quieter, more contemplative, and less crowded than the Terracotta Warriors.
 
 **Winner:** The Terracotta Warriors for sheer spectacle. Longmen Grottoes for artistic beauty.
 
 ### Secondary Attractions
 
 **Xi'an** has the ancient City Wall (14km, cycle it!), the Muslim Quarter (one of China's best food streets), the Shaanxi History Museum (top-3 in China), and the Big Wild Goose Pagoda.
 
 **Luoyang** has the Shaolin Temple (birthplace of kung fu, 1h away), the White Horse Temple (first Buddhist temple in China, 68 AD), the Luoyang Museum (excellent Bronze Age collection), and the annual Peony Festival (April).
 
 **Winner:** Xi'an for density of attractions within the city. Luoyang if you add the Shaolin Temple day trip.
 
 ### Practicalities
 
 **Xi'an** has direct flights from Beijing (2h), Shanghai (2.5h), and most major cities. The metro covers all attractions. The Muslim Quarter is walkable from the city centre. The Terracotta Warriors are 1h from the city by public bus.
 
 **Luoyang** doesn't have a commercial airport — you arrive by high-speed rail from Zhengzhou (40 min), Xi'an (2.5h), or Beijing (3.5h). The metro (Lines 1, 2) covers the city centre and Longmen Station. The Shaolin Temple is a 1h drive away.
 
 **Winner:** Xi'an — better connected and easier to navigate.
 
 ### Food
 
 **Xi'an** has arguably the best street food scene in northern China — the Muslim Quarter alone is worth a visit. Biang biang noodles, lamb paomo (bread soaked in lamb stew), cold noodles, and pomegranate juice.
 
 **Luoyang** has the famous Water Banquet (24-course soup-based banquet), Luoyang beef soup (the definitive local breakfast), and peony pastries. The food is excellent but less varied and accessible than Xi'an's Muslim Quarter.
 
 **Winner:** Xi'an — one of China's great food cities.

 ## FAQ
 
 ### Which is older — Xi'an or Luoyang?
 
 Both served as capitals for over 1,000 years across multiple dynasties. Luoyang was the capital during the Eastern Zhou, Eastern Han, and Tang Dynasty (as a secondary capital). Xi'an (Chang'an) was the capital during the Western Zhou, Qin, Han, and Tang Dynasty. The oldest continuous settlement is Luoyang (founded in the Xia Dynasty, ~2000 BC).
 
 ### Should I visit Luoyang or Xi'an first?
 
 Visit **Xi'an first** — it has more to see, better infrastructure, and makes a better base for a first-time China traveler. Then take the 2.5-hour train to Luoyang if you have 2–3 extra days.
