# Can You Drink the Water in China? Food & Water Safety for Travelers

**No. Tap water is not safe to drink anywhere in China.** This applies to five-star hotels, airport lounges, and mountain guesthouses equally. Boil it, filter it, or buy bottled. Here's everything else you need to know about staying healthy through food and water in China.

## Water: The Non-Negotiables

| Situation | Safe? | What to Do |
|-----------|-------|------------|
| **Tap water** | ❌ No | Never drink from the tap. Even in luxury hotels. Even in Beijing |
| **Boiled water** (开水 / kāishuǐ) | ✅ Yes | Every hotel room has an electric kettle. Boil water each night; fill your bottle in the morning. Chinese people have drunk boiled water for centuries — it's the traditional safety measure |
| **Bottled water** | ✅ Yes | ¥2-3 at any convenience store. Check the seal is intact |
| **Ice in reputable restaurants** | ⚠️ Usually | Chain restaurants and hotels use purified water for ice. Street stalls: skip the ice |
| **Brushing teeth** | ⚠️ | Use bottled or boiled water. A single rinse with tap water is unlikely to cause problems, but why risk it |

## Street Food: Safe If You're Smart

Chinese street food is one of the world's great culinary experiences — and it's generally safe if you follow two rules:

1. **Eat where locals eat.** A stall with a long queue has high turnover. Fresh ingredients. Nothing sits out.
2. **Food cooked to order is safest.** The *jianbing* (savory crêpe) made in front of you is inherently safe. The pre-cooked meat sitting in a warming tray at an empty stall is not.

Stalls you should trust: busy ones, cooking food from raw in front of you, with a line of Chinese customers. Stalls you should skip: empty ones during peak meal times, pre-cooked food that's been sitting out, and anything near tourist attractions that seems too clean.

## What About "Traveler's Diarrhea"?

Most travelers experience mild stomach adjustment in their first few days — not food poisoning, just your microbiome encountering entirely new bacteria. **Bring loperamide (Imodium) and take it only if you need to travel.** For mild discomfort: let it pass naturally. Your body is adapting.

Serious food poisoning is rare in China if you follow water and street food guidelines. The biggest risk factor is not exotic ingredients — it's food that's been sitting out too long at low-turnover establishments.

## What to Pack

- **Loperamide** (anti-diarrheal) — the single most important medication
- **Oral rehydration salts** — for if things get bad. Available at Chinese pharmacies too
- **Hand sanitizer** — use before eating street food
- **Tissue packs** — public bathrooms rarely have toilet paper
- **Reusable water bottle** — boil hotel water each night, fill in the morning. Saves ¥10-20/day and plastic waste

## The Bottom Line

Millions of travelers visit China every year and eat magnificently without getting sick. Follow the water rules (bottled or boiled, always), eat at busy street stalls, and carry tissues and hand sanitizer. Your stomach will thank you — and your taste buds will have the trip of a lifetime.
