# Is China Safe for Tourists? An Honest Answer

The short answer: **China is one of the safest countries in the world for tourists.** Violent crime against foreigners is virtually non-existent. You can walk city streets at midnight without concern. The biggest risks are practical, not criminal: traffic, air quality, and food hygiene.

But "is it safe?" is usually code for a deeper question. Let's answer the real ones.

## Crime in China — The Numbers

China's violent crime rate is a fraction of what it is in the US, UK, or Australia. Gun ownership is illegal for civilians. Street robbery targeting tourists is extremely rare. The most common crime affecting travelers is **pickpocketing** in crowded tourist areas — the Forbidden City, the Bund on weekends, Xi'an Muslim Quarter — and even that is less common than in European tourist cities.

## Solo Female Travel in China

China is one of the best countries for solo female travelers. Harassment on the street is very uncommon compared to many parts of Europe and South Asia. Women can travel alone comfortably, eat alone without being bothered, and navigate public transport safely at any hour. The main practical concerns: carry tissues (public toilets lack paper), use DiDi instead of street taxis at night (the app tracks your ride), and book hotels with foreign-guest licenses.

## Scams to Know About (So You Can Avoid Them)

| Scam | How It Works | What to Do |
|------|-------------|------------|
| **Tea House Scam** | Friendly English-speaking students invite you to a "traditional tea ceremony" — the bill is ¥500+ | Politely decline. Real tea houses have prices on the menu |
| **"Free" Art Exhibition** | Young artists invite you to see their work. You're pressured to buy overpriced paintings | Say "bù yào, xièxie" (不要，谢谢 / no thank you) and walk away |
| **Fake Terracotta Warriors** | Touts near Xi'an sell tickets to "another warriors site — closer, cheaper" | The only authentic site is 40km east of the city. Take only Bus 306 from the railway station |
| **Airport "Taxi"** | Unofficial drivers at arrivals charging 5x the meter fare | Use DiDi or the official taxi queue. Never accept rides from people approaching you in the terminal |

## The Real Risks (And How to Handle Them)

**Traffic:** Chinese drivers don't always yield to pedestrians. Cross streets assertively but carefully. Look both ways even on one-way streets — e-bikes come from everywhere.

**Air Quality:** Beijing and Xi'an in winter (Nov-Feb) can have unhealthy AQI levels. Check the AirVisual app. Wear an N95 mask on bad days. Southern cities (Kunming, Shenzhen, Xiamen) have clean air year-round.

**Food:** Chinese food is safe but your stomach may need time to adjust. Avoid tap water (not safe). Bottled water is cheap and everywhere. Street food from busy stalls with high turnover is generally fine.

**Language:** Most people you encounter won't speak English. Download offline translation packs before arriving. Save your hotel address in Chinese characters. [Language guide →](/country/language-guide)

## The Bottom Line

China will probably feel safer than your home country. The combination of low crime, ubiquitous CCTV, and a culture that doesn't tolerate street harassment makes it an exceptionally secure place to travel. The risks that exist are practical, measurable, and easy to prepare for. Come with common sense and a translation app. You'll be fine.
